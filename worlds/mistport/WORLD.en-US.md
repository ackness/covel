# Mistport: The Riftide Chronicle

## The World

Mistport is a harbor city jammed between a cliff face and the sea, like an iron wedge hammered into the rock. A pale grey fog lies over everything and never lifts. The air always tastes of salt and rust, and you can never see more than fifty paces. No one in Mistport has ever seen the sky; the old people say the fog last cleared three generations ago. Locals don't say "good morning." They say "quiet fog" — a quiet fog means the fog beasts are still far off.

The city climbs the cliff in three tiers. At the top sits the **Upper City**, where the Council and the merchant houses hold the clifftop behind high stone walls that keep the fog at their feet. Set into the dome of the Council Hall is an ancient tide-stone lamp that needs no flame. The middle tier is **Mid-Harbor**, a crush of docks, workshops, inns and gambling dens. Its mechanical clocktower runs on the tides and strikes once every six hours; it is the only way anyone in Mistport tells the time. At the bottom lies the **Undertow**. When the tide comes in it is drowned under grey seawater, and only when the tide goes out does it lay bare the ruins of an ancient civilization.

No one knows who built those ruins. The one thing anyone can be sure of is that every ebb uncovers a different layout, as if the seabed were rebuilding itself. The Tide-Reader Guild calls it the **Riftide** and still can't explain it. Relics lie scattered through the ruins: some light a hundred paces of fog, some turn your arm to stone at a touch, some whisper at night. The relic trade is what keeps Mistport alive, and it is where every quarrel in the city starts.

Worse, the fog is getting thicker. The most sensitive Tide-Readers can tell: over the past year, visibility has shrunk a little at a time, and the gaps between ebbs have grown a little longer. Some say it's a cycle. Some say it's an omen. And some say the fog is waking up.

## Core Rules

- **The law of the Riftide**: Every ebb uncovers a completely different set of ruins, and no layout ever repeats. No map is any use; every trip down is made blind. The window usually lasts four to six hours. The flood comes back very fast, and no one can predict when. Few who are caught by it ever return.
- **Fog beasts**: Shapeless creatures called fog beasts live in the fog, and sound and light draw them. An open flame, a raised voice, metal striking metal — any of these can bring them. Instead of torches, explorers carry tide-stone fog-lamps, made to give cold light and make little noise. In the Undertow, silence is the first rule of survival.
- **Relic appraisal**: Ancient relics are unpredictable and can kill. Using a relic before a Tide-Reader has appraised it is a serious crime — yet on the black market an unappraised relic sells for three times the price of an appraised one. The buyer is betting on the unknown.
- **The Tide-Seal Decree**: A Council law that bars civilians without a permit from the Undertow, on pain of prison and the loss of their property. It is hard to enforce. The Undertow is too big, the fog too thick, and the Council's own patrols often get lost down there.
- **Tide-Force and fog-rot**: A very few people can feel a strange energy rising from the ruins under the sea. It is called **Tide-Force**: strongest at ebb, almost gone at flood. Tide-Readers guide it with a technique called tide-sigils, and use it to steady relics. But Tide-Force has a price. Overuse brings on **fog-rot**: the body slowly turns translucent and finally dissolves into the fog, until no one can tell which part is the person and which is the fog.

## Factions

**The Mistport Council** is made up of merchant leaders and old families, and holds the Upper City and the legal relic trade. Its Speaker, Chen Yuanshan, is pushing hard for the Tide-Seal Decree. In public it's about keeping ordinary people safe; in practice it would corner the relic supply, since the Council's own expeditions are exempt. The Council is not of one mind. The doves argue a lockdown only feeds the black market; the hawks want the Salt Fangs cleared out by force. What Chen himself is after, not even his colleagues can say. His private expedition goes down far more often than "keeping order" would ever require.

**The Tide-Reader Guild** is the only scholarly body qualified to appraise ancient relics. It lives on Council money but stays independent in name. Its head, Guildmaster Qi, is a gentle old scholar who cares more about research than politics. The Guild's young Tide-Readers are growing uneasy: they have found things in the ruins, and the Guild's leadership seems to be deliberately burying certain appraisal reports. "Tide-Force does not exist; it is superstition," runs the Guild's official position. Yet more and more apprentices, deep in the ebb, have heard things they were never meant to hear.

**The Salt Fangs** run the illegal relic trade in the Undertow, and their roots go deep into the lower city. Their second-in-command, Iron Meg, keeps order with an iron hand, and she holds her people to one rule that never bends: never sell a dangerous, unappraised relic to a civilian who doesn't know what they're buying. The Salt Fangs and the Council have been locked in a cold war for years. Each plants spies in the other, now and then they fight in the alleys, and neither can finish the other off. The lower city has a saying: Meg doesn't run the black market just for the money.

**The Congregation of the Mist** is a mysterious sect that has risen in the last few years. It worships the **Mist Lord**: its faithful believe the fog itself is a conscious being, and the ruins of the Undertow are the Mist Lord's body. To the poor of the lower city it preaches that fog-rot is not a curse but ascension, and it has taken in a great many people with nowhere else to go. The Council calls it a cult, but its numbers are growing alarmingly fast — especially since its youngest Mist-Speaker, Frost, has named the time of the ebbs and the places ruins would surface, correctly, again and again.

## Key Figures

- **Lin Yuanzhou** — a new Tide-Reader apprentice who has just passed his first exam; careful, and endlessly curious. Since the exam something has been weighing on him; ask, and he says he hasn't been sleeping well. If the player is also an apprentice, he is their closest peer in the Guild; for an Undertow local, he is a newly met ally inside it. He may be the first to drag the player into truths no one was supposed to know.
- **Su Yao** — a full Tide-Reader and Guildmaster Qi's assistant. If the player took the Guild entrance exam, she was their examiner. She does everything by the book and is loyal to the Guild, but since Qi disappeared she has begun to doubt what she always believed. She is the player's bridge to the Guild's leadership, and many leads pass through her hands — but she always knows more than she says.
- **Iron Meg** — second-in-command of the Salt Fangs, in her thirties. A relic accident left her left arm fog-rotted from the elbow down; it is translucent now and gives off a faint grey glow. Cold and hard, she keeps her word, and she is the one who really keeps order in the lower city. She has been buying up one particular kind of relic fragment and pays far more than any trader would. What she wants them for, she won't say.
- **Chen Yuanshan (Speaker Chen)** — chair of the Council, past sixty, and a practiced political schemer. He pushes the Tide-Seal Decree in the name of public safety while he quietly sends his trusted expedition back down into the Undertow, again and again. What is he looking for? Those who know have either kept quiet or disappeared.
- **Guildmaster Qi** — head of the Tide-Reader Guild, a gentle white-haired scholar who knows more about the Riftide than anyone alive. Lately he is never without his gloves. As the story opens, he has left a letter and walked down into the Undertow at flood tide — when no one can get into the Undertow at all.
- **Frost** — the Congregation's youngest Mist-Speaker, fifteen or sixteen at most. She says she can hear the fog whispering, and more than once she has named the time of an ebb and the place a ruin would surface, and been right. Prophet, madwoman or fraud? She looks at the player as if she has known them for a long time.
- **Grey Falcon** — captain of Speaker Chen's private expedition, and the sharpest blade the Tide-Seal Decree has. Silent, efficient, not a wasted feeling. On orders, he seals off particular areas, searches for particular types of relic, and arrests a few people "nobody has ever heard of." Lately the trail he is following leads toward the place where Qi disappeared.

## Your Way In

The player isn't the lone savior of some prophecy. The player is a new variable that knocks seven people off the paths they were already on. The opening fits either of two backgrounds:

- **New Tide-Reader apprentice**: your exam left a faint tide-sigil on your wrist, and you carry a Guild tide-stone fog-lamp and apprentice archive slip. Su Yao was your examiner, and Lin Yuanzhou sat the exam with you. You can enter the Guild Tower lawfully, but you are expected to obey the Guild's rules on sealed records and appraisals.
- **Undertow local**: you know the Drawdown Steps, the hidden hatch in the pier, and the fog-lamp hand signals, and carry an old cold-light fog-lamp plus waterproof oilcloth. Qi once appraised a relic for you or your family free of charge; before he vanished he left Su Yao a note asking her to find "someone who remembers the way and doesn't put blind faith in maps." You have no permit, but you find it easier to get close to the Salt Fangs and the Congregation.

Whatever your background, Su Yao has only half a letter when she finds you on the Main Pier. The other half, the route Qi actually took, and the reason the deep ebb came early all have to be pieced together from the cracks between what different people want. Lin Yuanzhou needs someone to keep his tide sense quiet. Iron Meg has to beat Chen to the Key. Grey Falcon needs to know whether the orders he's carrying have been altered. Frost wants to find out whether the player really does "hear it too." These people will ask you for promises, and they will refuse anything that goes against their side. Keep your word, lie, get hurt, choose — and their trust, and where they are, will shift. They don't stand still waiting for you to bring them a quest.

## The Case Board and Consequences

The first four leads are connected, but none of them proves anything alone:

- **The torn letter** is in Su Yao's hands. It reads: "The Key must not fall into his hands." Its edge is crusted with black tide-salt, which crystallizes only in the ruins the ebb uncovers — yet someone saw Qi leave at high tide.
- **Appraisal Report 74** is missing a page in the Guild's Sealed Archive; the catalog still lists a "hinged-tooth grey fragment." On the same day, a page was removed from the visitor ledger without leaving a mark.
- **Three traces in the Echo Gallery**: Qi's tide-sigil, new rope from Grey Falcon's private unit, and a copper plate engraved with a nursery rhyme. The times they could have been left contradict one another; sorting them out means comparing tide lines and testimony.
- **The survivor in the Stilltide Asylum** is the only one who came back from the deep-dive team three years ago. The Council keeps him isolated, and all he says is "It woke up down there" — but the ward records show those were not the words he said first.

A lead moves through four states: unverified, corroborated, contaminated, resolved. Finding physical evidence only makes it unverified. Independent testimony or a tide-sigil record can corroborate it. Floodwater, forgery, or a leak can contaminate it. The tide washing evidence away doesn't prove anyone's story.

A deep ebb runs in this order: the triple warning; the sea falling past the ninetieth of the Drawdown Steps; the ruins fully exposed; the flood bell; the sudden flood. After the flood bell there is usually only half an hour to get out. Every time the player slips past a checkpoint, uses a relic, accepts a permit or ends up owing someone for the pier hatch, it should leave at least one visible consequence: the tide moves on, the location changes, vitality or stress shifts, fog-rot rises, someone's attitude changes, a faction's permit or warrant escalates, or confidence in a lead changes.

Fog-rot cannot be undone; it is measured in degrees. Below twenty-five there are usually only hidden signs, such as always feeling cold or a ringing in the ears. From twenty-five to forty-nine, patches of the body turn translucent. From fifty to seventy-four, Tide-Force feels stronger, but lasting impairment comes with it. At seventy-five and above, the body may dissolve into the fog at any moment. Rest restores vitality and lowers stress; it never lowers fog-rot.

## The Story

The story forces no single ending, but it falls naturally into three acts, and the deeper it goes, the higher the price:

1. **Prologue: The Ebb** — A deep ebb is coming, rarer than most. As a Tide-Reader apprentice (or an Undertow local), the player gets pulled into Qi's disappearance, searches for him among the Council, the Guild, the Salt Fangs and the Congregation, and learns how to stay alive in the fog.
2. **The Fight for the Fragments** — Slowly the player realizes that Qi's disappearance, Meg's fragments and Chen's searches all point to the same thing: a "Key" that can open the deepest part of the Undertow. The four factions start fighting over it, and the player has to pick a side. Every choice costs something that can never be taken back: favors, fog-rot, trust, lives.
3. **The Mist Lord Awakens** — The fog thickens and the cycle nears its tipping point. In the end the player reaches the truth everyone avoids naming: something deep in the Undertow is waking up. Whether to stop it, wake it, or learn to live alongside it depends on every choice the player has made along the way.

## Hidden Threads

- No human has ever reached the deepest part of the Undertow. In the last deep ebb, an expedition brought back a relic carved with words identical to the inscription on Mistport's founding stone — yet carbon dating makes it ten thousand years older than Mistport.
- The number of people with fog-rot has tripled over the past year, and the authorities refuse to admit it. Qi's private notes hold one line: "The fog is thickening. It thickens once every ten years. We are at the tipping point of some cycle."
- The relic fragments Meg collects look like the parts of a "Key." What does it open? She won't say. But Speaker Chen is looking for the same thing, and it is on Grey Falcon's search list too.
- Three years ago, a Council expedition went deep into the Undertow and never came back. The one survivor went mad and says only one thing, over and over: "It woke up down there." Where is that man being held now?
- Why do Frost's prophecies always come true? Is the fog really speaking to her, or is someone speaking through her? The first time she met the player she said, "You can hear it too, can't you?" What did she mean?
- Qi never used Tide-Force openly, so why does he have fog-rot? If not Tide-Force, what else could make a person slowly dissolve into the fog?
- What the cast keeps hidden; don't say it outright before the story uncovers it. Qi is one of the few who know the truth about the Riftide and chose to say nothing; fog-rot is already showing on his hands. Iron Meg trained once as a Tide-Reader apprentice — her deepest secret. During his exam, Lin Yuanzhou found he can faintly sense Tide-Force, and he tells no one, because the Guild's official position is that Tide-Force doesn't exist. Grey Falcon has started to wonder what lies behind his orders.

## Opening Scene

Dusk. You step onto the wet planks of the main pier in Mid-Harbor. The ferry's mooring line fades into the fog behind you, as if you'd come from nowhere at all. The air is thick and salt-heavy. Faintly, the clocktower tolls — not the usual time signal, but three quick strikes. Along the dock, the old sailors stop what they're doing and look at each other.

Three strikes mean a **deep ebb warning**, and that almost never happens. The last deep ebb was six years ago. It uncovered a huge structure now called the **Bone Arch**, and it cost seventeen lives.

The crowd on the pier splits in two. In the shadows the Salt Fangs are recruiting anyone bold enough to go down, paying well. Council patrols are posting an emergency Tide-Seal Decree: anyone entering the Undertow without a permit will be arrested.

A young woman in a Tide-Reader Guild uniform shoulders through the crowd and comes straight at you. If you sat the entrance exam, you know her — Su Yao, your examiner. If you're an Undertow local, the corner of a note is sticking out of her sleeve, and the name on it is the one Qi used for you. She doesn't look well.

If you are a new apprentice, her eyes fall on the tide-sigil left by your exam. If you are an Undertow local, she draws Old Qi's note for you from her sleeve.

"Guildmaster Qi is missing." She keeps her voice low and doesn't wait for you to speak. "He left a letter this morning and was gone. It says he went down into the Undertow — but the tide was coming in. No one can get into the Undertow on a flood tide."

She glances toward the clocktower. "But if the deep ebb really is coming—"

By old habit a deep-ebb warning stops at three; today an unscheduled fourth strike rings out heavy as iron, as if something beneath the seabed pushed the gears. The ground shivers. At the edge of the dock the sea is pulling back fast enough to see, and beneath it the outline of ruins no one has ever laid eyes on, glowing a dull grey.

And somewhere in the fog, something seems to open its eyes.
