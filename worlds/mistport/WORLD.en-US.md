# Mistport Chronicles

## The World

Mistport is a port city wedged between a sheer sea cliff and a restless ocean, clinging to the rock like an iron wedge driven into stone. A perpetual grey-white fog smothers everything; the air always tastes of brine and corroded iron, and visibility never reaches past fifty paces. No one has seen Mistport's sky — the old folk say the last time the fog lifted was three generations ago. The locals don't say "good morning"; they say "quiet fog," because a quiet fog means the fog beasts aren't close.

The city climbs the cliff in three vertical tiers. At the top is the **Upper City**, where the Council and the great merchant houses perch behind stone walls that keep the fog below; the ancient tide-stone lamp set in the Council Hall's dome is the only light in the city that burns without flame. The middle tier, **Mid-Harbor**, is a crowded tangle of docks, workshops, inns, and gambling dens, where a mechanical clocktower driven by the tides tolls every six hours — the only way Mistport keeps time. Lowest of all lies the **Undertow**, drowned beneath grey water at high tide, exposing the ruins of an ancient civilization only when the sea draws back.

No one knows who built the ruins. The one certainty is that the Undertow never repeats: every low tide reveals a completely different layout, as if the seabed rearranges itself between floods. The Tide-Reader Guild calls this phenomenon the **Riftide**, and after generations of study still cannot explain it. Scattered through the ruins are ancient relics — some can light a hundred paces through the fog, some turn an arm to stone at a touch, some whisper softly in the dark. The relic trade is Mistport's lifeblood, and the root of every conflict in the city.

More unsettling still: the fog is thickening. The most sensitive Tide-Readers can feel it — visibility shrinking inch by inch this past year, the intervals between ebbs stretching longer. Some call it a cycle, some an omen, and some say the fog is waking up.

## Core Rules

- **The Riftide Law**: Every ebb exposes an entirely different ruin structure, never repeating. No map is ever valid twice; every descent is a blind one. The withdrawal window lasts four to six hours, and the flood returns fast and unpredictable — those caught in the rising tide rarely come back.
- **Fog Beasts**: Formless creatures dwell in the mist, drawn to sound and light. Open flame, shouting, the clash of metal — any of it can call them. Explorers carry cold tide-stone lanterns (dim, silent light) and speak in whispers, if at all. In the Undertow, silence is the first law of survival.
- **Relic Appraisal**: Ancient relics have effects that cannot be guessed from their appearance, and they may be deadly. Using an unappraised relic without a Tide-Reader is a serious crime — yet on the black market unappraised relics sell for triple the price, because the unknown is the attraction.
- **The Seal Tide Decree**: Council law forbidding unlicensed civilians from entering the Undertow, on pain of imprisonment and seizure. Enforcement is difficult — the Undertow is too vast and the fog too thick, and Council patrols often get lost down there themselves.
- **Tide-Force and Fog-Rot**: A rare few can sense a strange energy rising from the deep ruins — "Tide-Force," strongest at ebb and all but gone at flood. Tide-Readers channel it through "tide-marks" to stabilize relics. But the power has a price: overuse brings **fog-rot**, the body turning translucent and slowly dissolving into the mist, until no one can tell which part is still a person and which part is fog.

## Factions

**The Mistport Council** — merchant leaders and noble houses, controlling the Upper City and the legal relic trade. Councilor Chen is pushing the Seal Tide Decree with everything he has — officially for public safety, in truth to monopolize the relic supply, and the Council's own expeditions are exempt from it. The Council is fracturing: doves argue suppression only feeds the black market; hawks want the Salt Fangs crushed by force. No one, not even his colleagues, can read what Chen really wants: his private expeditions search the Undertow far more often than keeping the peace could ever require.

**The Tide-Reader Guild** — the only scholarly body qualified to appraise relics, funded by Council coin yet nominally independent. Guildmaster Qi is a gentle, aging scholar who cares more for knowledge than politics. The younger Tide-Readers grow restless: they have found things in the ruins, and the upper ranks seem to be deliberately burying certain appraisal reports. "Tide-Force is mere superstition," runs the official line — yet more and more apprentices, in the depths of the ebb, hear what they were never meant to hear.

**The Salt Fangs** — they control the Undertow's black market with roots sunk deep into the lower tiers. Their second-in-command, **Iron Meg**, runs operations with an iron fist and one unbreakable rule: never sell an unappraised, dangerous relic to a civilian who doesn't know what they're buying. The Salt Fangs and the Council live in a cold war of mutual infiltration and back-alley violence, neither able to destroy the other. The lower tiers have a saying: Meg's grip on the market is not only about coin.

**The Congregation of the Mist** — a religious movement risen in recent years that worships "the Mist Lord," believing the fog itself is a living, conscious entity and the Undertow ruins are fragments of its body. They draw in the desperate and dispossessed with the promise that fog-rot is not a curse but a transcendence. The Council brands them a cult; their following grows at an unsettling pace — especially after their youngest Mist-Speaker, Frost, predicted the timing of ebbs and the location of ruins again and again, every time correct.

## Key Figures

- **Lin Yuanzhou** — a newly admitted Tide-Reader apprentice, cautious yet fiercely curious. Since his exam he has been preoccupied; ask him, and he says he slept badly. He becomes the player's closest peer in the Guild, and may well be the first to pull the player into truths better left buried.
- **Su Yao** — a fully fledged Tide-Reader, Guildmaster Qi's assistant, and the invigilator at the player's entrance exam. Meticulous and loyal to the Guild, she begins to doubt everything she believed once Qi vanishes. She is the player's bridge to the Guild's upper ranks, and many quest threads pass through her hands — but she always knows more than she says.
- **Iron Meg** — second-in-command of the Salt Fangs, mid-thirties, her left arm rotted translucent from the elbow down by a relic accident, glowing faintly grey. Cold, precise, and true to her word, she is the real keeper of order in the lower city. She keeps buying one kind of relic fragment at prices that make no sense as trade — what for, she won't say.
- **Councilor Chen (Chen Yuanshan)** — Council chairman, past sixty, a master of political maneuver. He pushes the Seal Tide Decree in the name of public safety while sending his expeditions back into the Undertow again and again. What is he searching for? Those who know the answer are either silent, or already gone.
- **Guildmaster Qi** — head of the Tide-Reader Guild, a white-haired, soft-spoken scholar who understands the Riftide better than anyone alive. Lately he is never seen without gloves. As the story opens, he leaves a letter and walks into the Undertow at high tide, when no one can enter the Undertow at all.
- **Frost** — the Congregation's youngest Mist-Speaker, barely sixteen. She claims to hear the fog's whispers, and has predicted ebb timing and ruin locations more than once. Prophet, lunatic, or fraud? The way she looks at the player, it is as if she already knows them.
- **Grey Falcon** — captain of Councilor Chen's private expedition, and the sharpest instrument of the Seal Tide Decree. Silent, efficient, without a wasted emotion, he seals off certain zones, hunts certain relics, and arrests people "no one has ever heard of." Lately his quarry points toward where Qi vanished.

## Player Origins and Ensemble Entry

The player is not a singular savior named by prophecy, but a new variable capable of bending seven existing lives off course. The opening naturally supports two origins:

- **New Tide-Reader apprentice** — the entrance trial left a faint tide-mark on your wrist. Su Yao examined you and Lin Yuanzhou is your peer. You may enter the Guild Tower lawfully, but are expected to obey its sealed archives and appraisal discipline.
- **Undertow local** — you know the Drawdown Steps, the pier hatch, and fog-lamp hand signs. Qi once appraised a relic for you or your family without charge. Before vanishing, he left Su a note asking for “someone who remembers routes and does not trust maps.” You have no permit, but reach the Salt Fangs and the Congregation more easily.

Whichever your origin, Su finds you on the Main Pier with only half a letter. Its other half, Qi's real route, and the reason the deep withdrawal came early must be assembled from gaps between other people's interests. Lin needs someone to keep his Tide Sense secret; Iron Meg needs Chen stopped from completing the Key first; Grey Falcon needs to know whether his orders were altered; Frost wants proof that the player truly “also hears.” They ask for promises, refuse requests that violate their positions, and change trust and whereabouts when the player keeps faith, lies, suffers, or chooses — they do not wait in place like quest dispensers.

## Opening Caseboard and State

The first four leads connect, but none proves a conclusion alone:

- **The torn letter** is held by Su and reads, “The Key must not fall into his hands.” Its edge bears black tide-salt that crystallizes only in exposed ruins, though witnesses saw Qi leave at high tide.
- **Appraisal Report 74** is missing one page from the Sealed Archive; the catalog still describes a “hinged-tooth grey fragment.” A page also vanished cleanly from that day's visitor ledger.
- **Three traces in the Echo Gallery** belong to Qi's tide-mark, fresh rope from Falcon's unit, and a copper plate engraved with a nursery rhyme. Their apparent times contradict one another and require tide-lines and testimony to cross-check.
- **The Stilltide survivor** is the only person to return from the expedition three years ago. The Council keeps him isolated; he says only, “It woke up down there,” but ward records show those were not his first words.

Clues move through unverified, corroborated, contaminated, and resolved states. Finding an object makes it unverified; independent testimony or tide-mark records can corroborate it; floodwater, forgery, or a public leak can contaminate it. The tide removing evidence never proves anyone's interpretation.

The deep withdrawal advances from triple warning, to water passing step ninety, to full exposure, return bell, and sudden flood. After the return bell, usually only half an hour remains. Every bypassed checkpoint, activated relic, accepted permit, or favor owed for the pier hatch should leave at least one visible consequence: tide-time advancing, location changing, vitality or stress shifting, fog-rot rising, a person's attitude changing, a faction permit or warrant escalating, or confidence in a clue changing.

Fog-rot never reverses. Below twenty-five it usually shows only as cold sensitivity or ringing ears; from twenty-five to forty-nine a body part turns translucent; from fifty to seventy-four Tide-Force grows clearer while lasting impairment sets in; above seventy-five dissolution into the fog may come at any time. Rest can restore vitality and reduce stress, but never reduce fog-rot.

## Story Arcs

The story forces no single ending, but falls naturally into three stages — and the deeper you go, the higher the price:

1. **The Ebbing Prologue** — A rare deep withdrawal approaches. As a Tide-Reader apprentice (or an Undertow dweller), the player is drawn into Guildmaster Qi's disappearance, searching for him among Council, Guild, Salt Fangs, and Congregation while learning to survive in the fog.
2. **The Fight for the Fragments** — The player slowly realizes that Qi's disappearance, Meg's fragments, and Chen's search all point to the same thing: a "key" that opens the deepest reaches of the Undertow. The four powers grasp for it, and the player must choose a side — every choice leaving an irreversible cost: favors, fog-rot, trust, lives.
3. **The Mist Lord Awakens** — The fog thickens; the cycle nears its critical point. The player will finally touch the truth everyone refuses to speak: something deep in the Undertow is waking. To stop it, wake it, or live alongside it depends on every choice made along the way.

## Threads & Mysteries

- The deepest reaches of the Undertow have never been touched by human hands. In the last deep withdrawal an expedition brought back a relic carved with text identical to Mistport's founding stele — yet carbon dating puts it ten thousand years older than the city.
- Cases of fog-rot have tripled in the past year, and the Council refuses to admit it. In Qi's private journal: "The fog is thickening. Once every decade. We stand at the critical point of some cycle."
- Meg's relic fragments look like the parts of a "key." What does it open? She won't say. But Councilor Chen seeks the same thing, and it is on Grey Falcon's search list too.
- Three years ago a Council expedition vanished entirely in the deep Undertow. The lone survivor went mad and can only repeat one line: "It woke up down there." Where is that person held now?
- Why do Frost's prophecies always come true? Is the fog truly speaking to her — or is someone speaking through her? The first time she saw the player, she said, "You can hear it too, can't you?" What did she mean?
- Qi never publicly used Tide-Force, so why is he rotting? If not Tide-Force, what else could dissolve a person slowly into the fog?
- What the cast hides; do not state it before the story reveals it: Qi is one of the few who know the truth of the Riftide and has chosen silence, and fog-rot has already marked his hands; Iron Meg was once a Tide-Reader apprentice, her greatest secret; Lin Yuanzhou found in his exam that he can faintly sense Tide-Force and dares tell no one, since the Guild's official line is that it does not exist; Grey Falcon has begun to question what lies behind his orders.

## Opening Scene

Dusk. You step onto the slick planking of Mid-Harbor's main pier. The ferry's mooring line vanishes into the fog, as if you had walked out of nothing. The air is heavy with salt. In the distance, the clocktower tolls dully.

Then the bell rings — not the regular timekeeping toll, but three sharp strikes in rapid succession. On the dock, grizzled sailors freeze mid-motion and trade uneasy glances. Three strikes mean a **deep withdrawal alert**, an extremely rare event. The last deep withdrawal was six years ago; it exposed the huge structure called the **Bone Arch**, and took seventeen lives.

The pier splits into two currents of humanity. In the shadows, Salt Fang recruiters sign up the bold with promises of rich pay, while Council officers plaster emergency Seal Tide Decree notices on every surface, warning that unauthorized Undertow entry means immediate arrest.

A young woman in a Tide-Reader Guild uniform pushes through the crowd toward you. If you sat the entrance trial, you recognize its examiner, Su Yao; if you are an Undertow local, the note showing at her sleeve bears the name Qi used for you. She looks rattled.

Her eyes catch the fresh apprentice tide-mark on your wrist. If instead you wear an Undertow local's waterproof knot, she draws a note bearing your origin from her sleeve.

“Guildmaster Qi is missing.” She does not wait for you to speak; her voice drops. “He left a note this morning and walked into the Undertow — but the tide was still in. No one can enter the Undertow during high tide.”

She glances toward the clocktower. "Unless a deep withdrawal changes the timing…"

The fourth bell rings. The ground trembles. At the edge of the pier the grey water begins to retreat, baring beneath it the grey-glowing outline of ruins no one has ever seen.

And in the fog, something seems to open its eyes.
