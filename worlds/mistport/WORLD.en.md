# Mistport Chronicles

## The World

Mistport is a port city wedged between a sheer sea cliff and a restless ocean, clinging to the rock like an iron wedge driven into stone. A perpetual grey-white fog smothers everything; the air always tastes of brine and corroded iron, and visibility never reaches past fifty paces. No one has seen Mistport's sky — the old folk say the last time the fog lifted was three generations ago. The locals don't say "good morning"; they say "quiet fog," because a quiet fog means the fog beasts aren't close.

The city climbs the cliff in three vertical tiers. At the top is the **Upper City**, where the Council and the great merchant houses perch behind stone walls thick enough to muffle the sea; the ancient tide-stone lamp set in the Council Hall's dome is the only light in the city that burns without flame. The middle tier, **Mid-Harbor**, is the city's beating heart — a chaotic tangle of docks, workshops, taverns, and gambling dens, where a great tide-driven clocktower marks time not by hours but by tidal cycles, the only schedule that matters. Lowest of all lies the **Undertow**, drowned beneath grey water at high tide, exposing the ruins of an ancient civilization only when the sea draws back.

No one knows who built the ruins. The one certainty is that the Undertow never repeats: every low tide reveals a completely different layout, as if the seabed rearranges itself between floods. The Tide-Reader Guild calls this phenomenon the **Riftide**, and after generations of study still cannot explain it. Scattered through the ruins are ancient relics — some can light a hundred paces through the fog, some turn an arm to stone at a touch, some whisper softly in the dark. The relic trade is Mistport's lifeblood, and the root of every conflict in the city.

More unsettling still: the fog is thickening. The most sensitive Tide-Readers can feel it — visibility shrinking inch by inch this past year, the intervals between ebbs stretching longer. Some call it a cycle, some an omen, and some say the fog is waking up.

## Core Rules

- **The Riftide Law**: Every ebb exposes an entirely different ruin structure, never repeating. No map is ever valid twice; every descent is a blind one. The withdrawal window lasts four to six hours, and the flood returns fast and unpredictable — those caught in the rising tide rarely come back.
- **Fog Beasts**: Formless creatures dwell in the mist, drawn to sound and light. Open flame, shouting, the clash of metal — any of it can call them within minutes. Explorers carry cold tide-stone lanterns (dim, silent light) and speak in whispers, if at all. In the Undertow, silence is the first law of survival.
- **Relic Appraisal**: Ancient relics have effects that cannot be guessed from their appearance — a jeweled dagger may be harmless, a plain stone may level a building. Using an unappraised relic without a Tide-Reader is a capital offense — yet on the black market unappraised relics sell for triple the price, because the unknown is the attraction.
- **The Seal Tide Decree**: Council law forbidding unlicensed civilians from entering the Undertow, on pain of imprisonment and seizure. Enforcement is spotty — the Undertow is too vast and the fog too thick for patrols to cover — so in practice the Decree mostly funnels all legal relic trade through Council-approved channels.
- **Tide-Force and Fog-Rot**: A rare few can sense a strange energy rising from the deep ruins — "Tide-Force," strongest at ebb and all but gone at flood. Tide-Readers channel it through "tide-marks" to stabilize relics. But the power has a price: overuse brings **fog-rot**, the body turning translucent and slowly dissolving into the mist, until no one can tell which part is still a person and which part is fog. The Council denies Tide-Force exists. The Guild officially agrees. Both are lying.

## Factions

**The Mistport Council** — merchant aristocrats who have held power for generations, controlling the Upper City and the legal relic trade. Councilor Chen is pushing the Seal Tide Decree harder than any predecessor, officially for public safety, while his private expeditions work the Undertow with impunity. The Council is fracturing: doves argue suppression only feeds the black market; hawks want the Salt Fangs crushed by force. Chen plays both sides, and no one is sure what he actually wants — his expeditions search the Undertow with an urgency that goes far beyond profit.

**The Tide-Reader Guild** — the only body qualified to appraise relics and study the Riftide, funded by Council coin yet nominally independent. Guildmaster Qi is a gentle, aging scholar who cares more for knowledge than politics, and one of the few alive who knows the truth of the Riftide — yet he has chosen silence. The younger Tide-Readers grow restless: several have noticed certain appraisal reports being buried by senior members, and relics of specific descriptions quietly confiscated rather than catalogued. "Tide-Force is mere superstition," runs the official line — yet more and more apprentices, in the depths of the ebb, hear what they were never meant to hear.

**The Salt Fangs** — they control the Undertow's black market from hidden caves below the city, roots sunk deep into the lower tiers. Their second-in-command, **Iron Meg**, runs operations with an iron fist and one unbreakable rule: never sell an unappraised, dangerous relic to a civilian who doesn't know what they're buying. She was once a Tide-Reader apprentice — a secret that could ruin her in the underworld and the Guild alike. The Salt Fangs and the Council live in a cold war of mutual infiltration and back-alley violence, neither able to destroy the other. Few know that Meg's grip on the market is not only about coin: she is quietly gathering specific relic fragments, pieces of something larger.

**The Congregation of the Mist** — a religious movement risen in recent years that worships "the Mist Lord," believing the fog itself is a living, conscious entity and the Undertow ruins are fragments of its body. They draw in the desperate and dispossessed with the promise that fog-rot is not a curse but a transcendence. The Council brands them a cult; their numbers have tripled in two years — especially after their youngest Mist-Speaker, Frost, predicted the timing of ebbs and the location of ruins again and again, every time correct.

## Key Figures

- **Lin Yuanzhou** — a newly admitted Tide-Reader apprentice, cautious yet fiercely curious. During his exam he discovered he can faintly sense Tide-Force — a secret he dares tell no one, since the Guild's official line is that Tide-Force does not exist. He becomes the player's closest peer in the Guild, and may well be the first to pull the player into truths better left buried.
- **Su Yao** — a fully fledged Tide-Reader, Guildmaster Qi's assistant, and the invigilator at the player's entrance exam. Meticulous and loyal to the Guild, she begins to doubt everything she believed once Qi vanishes. She is the player's bridge to the Guild's upper ranks, and many quest threads pass through her hands — but she always knows more than she says.
- **Iron Meg** — second-in-command of the Salt Fangs, mid-thirties, her left arm rotted translucent from the elbow down by a relic accident, glowing faintly grey. Cold, precise, and true to her word, she is the real keeper of order in the lower city. The relic fragments she gathers seem to be parts of a "key" — what it opens, she won't say; but Councilor Chen is hunting the same thing.
- **Councilor Chen (Chen Yuanshan)** — Council chairman, past sixty, a master politician who has outlasted three rivals and two assassination attempts. He pushes the Seal Tide Decree in the name of public safety while sending his expeditions back into the Undertow again and again. What is he searching for? Those who know the answer are either silent, or already gone.
- **Guildmaster Qi** — head of the Tide-Reader Guild, a white-haired, soft-spoken scholar who knows the Riftide's truth and keeps it sealed behind his lips. Lately the marks of fog-rot have appeared on his hands — though he has never publicly used Tide-Force. As the story opens, he leaves a letter and walks into the Undertow at high tide, when no one can enter the Undertow at all.
- **Frost** — the Congregation's youngest Mist-Speaker, barely sixteen. She claims to hear the fog's whispers, and has predicted ebb timing and ruin locations more than once. Prophet, lunatic, or fraud? The way she looks at the player, it is as if she already knows them.
- **Grey Falcon** — captain of Councilor Chen's private expedition, and the sharpest instrument of the Seal Tide Decree. Silent, efficient, without a wasted emotion, he seals off certain zones, hunts certain relics, and arrests people "no one has ever heard of." Lately his quarry points toward where Qi vanished — and he, too, has begun to question what lies behind his orders.

## Story Arcs

The story forces no single ending, but falls naturally into three stages — and the deeper you go, the higher the price:

1. **The Ebbing Prologue** — A rare deep withdrawal approaches. As a Tide-Reader apprentice (or an Undertow dweller), the player is drawn into Guildmaster Qi's disappearance, searching for him among Council, Guild, Salt Fangs, and Congregation while learning to survive in the fog.
2. **The Fight for the Fragments** — The player slowly realizes that Qi's disappearance, Meg's fragments, and Chen's search all point to the same thing: a "key" that opens the deepest reaches of the Undertow. The four powers grasp for it, and the player must choose a side — every choice leaving an irreversible cost: favors, fog-rot, trust, lives.
3. **The Mist Lord Awakens** — The fog thickens; the cycle nears its critical point. The player will finally touch the truth everyone refuses to speak: something deep in the Undertow is waking. To stop it, wake it, or live alongside it depends on every choice made along the way.

## Threads & Mysteries

- The deepest reaches of the Undertow have never been touched by human hands. In the last deep withdrawal an expedition brought back a relic carved with text identical to Mistport's founding stele — yet carbon dating puts it ten thousand years older than the city.
- Cases of fog-rot have tripled in the past year, and the Council refuses to admit it. In Qi's private journal: "The fog is thickening. Once every decade. We stand at the critical point of some cycle."
- Meg's relic fragments look like the parts of a "key." What does it open? She won't say. But Councilor Chen seeks the same thing, and it is on Grey Falcon's search list too.
- Three years ago a Council expedition vanished entirely in the deep Undertow. The lone survivor went mad and can only repeat one line: "It woke up down there." Where is that person held now?
- Why do Frost's prophecies always come true? Is the fog truly speaking to her — or is someone speaking through her? The first time she saw the player, she said, "You can hear it too, can't you?" What did she mean?
- Qi never publicly used Tide-Force, so why is he rotting? If not Tide-Force, what else could dissolve a person slowly into the fog?

## Opening Scene

Dusk. You step onto the slick planking of Mid-Harbor's main pier. The ferry's mooring line vanishes into grey nothing within arm's reach, as if the ship you just left has already ceased to exist. The air is thick enough to chew — salt, rust, the faint sweetness of something rotting. Somewhere above, the great tide-clock groans through its mechanisms.

Then the bell rings — not the regular timekeeping toll, but three sharp strikes in rapid succession. On the dock, grizzled sailors freeze mid-motion and trade uneasy glances. Three strikes mean a **deep withdrawal alert**: the tide pulling back farther than usual, baring ruins that haven't seen air in years. The last deep withdrawal was six years ago. It revealed the structure called the **Bone Arch**. It also killed seventeen people.

The pier splits into two currents of humanity. To your left, Salt Fang recruiters whisper promises of fortune to anyone brave or desperate enough to descend. To your right, Council officers plaster emergency Seal Tide Decree notices on every surface, warning that unauthorized Undertow entry means immediate arrest.

A young woman in a Tide-Reader Guild uniform pushes through the crowd toward you — you recognize her as the invigilator from your entrance exam days ago: Su Yao. She looks rattled.

"You're the new apprentice, the one who arrived today?" She doesn't wait for an answer; her voice drops. "Guildmaster Qi is missing. He left a note this morning and walked into the Undertow — but the tide was still in. No one can enter the Undertow during high tide."

She glances toward the clocktower. "Unless a deep withdrawal changes the timing…"

The fourth bell rings. The ground trembles. At the edge of the pier the grey water begins to retreat, baring beneath it the grey-glowing outline of ruins no one has ever seen.

And in the fog, something seems to open its eyes.
