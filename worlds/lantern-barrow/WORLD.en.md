# Lantern Barrow: First Expedition

Greyreed sits at the edge of the Greyreed Marsh: thirty-odd households, a mill, a tavern, and a small chapel. The hill north of the village is called the Lantern Barrow. For a hundred years the lamp on its crown has burned every night, tended by one keeper after another. Every child in Greyreed grows up knowing two rules: don't climb the hill after dark, and if the lantern ever goes out, tell a grown-up at once.

Three days ago, the lantern went out.

That night Keeper Ysolde took her oil lamp and walked through the bronze doors that had opened in the side of the barrow. She has not come out. Since then sheep have gone missing outside the village, the marsh fog has turned cold, and the miller's daughter says she hears singing under the ground at night — singing her dead grandfather's name. Reeve Aldous Penn has nailed a bounty notice up in the Crooked Antler: thirty silver to whoever goes down and brings the keeper home.

This is a classic tabletop adventure: take the job at the tavern, form a party, spend your points, roll your way down. You are an adventurer who has just arrived in Greyreed.

## Greyreed

- **The Crooked Antler** — the village's only tavern and inn. Mirelle Voss, the owner, has a long memory and a closed mouth, and every rumor crosses her bar first. A night's room and board costs two silver.
- **The Reeve's House** — a two-story stone house with the lord's crest over the door. The reeve wants this settled before the lord's letter arrives.
- **The Keeper's Cottage** — a stone cottage at the foot of the barrow hill, Ysolde's home for forty years: lamp oil, dozens of old logbooks, and a door that was never locked. The latest logbook stops three days before the lantern went dark.
- **The Mill** — the miller's daughter was the first to hear the singing.
- **The Dawn Chapel** — a small chapel to the light, empty for years until Sister Wren moved in yesterday.

## People

- **Brannoc Hale** — a fifty-year-old retired soldier who has been drinking quietly in the tavern corner for three days. He says he is only passing through, but he keeps watching the barrow hill. Twenty years ago he went into this barrow with a small company, and only he walked out.
- **Sister Wren** — a nineteen-year-old acolyte of the Order of the Dawn Lantern, sent to inspect the lantern and three days too late. She treats wounds, reads the old lantern script, and is hiding a question about her faith.
- **Mirelle Voss** — the innkeeper. Shrewd and warm, she hands you the truth only once she is sure you are worth it.
- **Reeve Aldous Penn** — Greyreed's headman. Respectable, anxious, and vague about the days before the keeper vanished.
- **Keeper Ysolde** — in her seventies, keeper of the lantern for forty years. She went into the barrow alone the night it went dark.
- **Corvin Ashe** — a young scholar from the capital who arrived a month ago asking everyone about the lantern. He checked out of the tavern the day before it went dark. No one saw him leave.

Brannoc and Wren can join your party. They have their own judgment, opinions, and secrets; they act on their own when things get dangerous, and they remember how you treated them.

## The Lantern Barrow

The barrow is bigger inside than the hill. Only the entrance is known:

- **The Bronze Doors** — open since the night the lantern died. The seal was pushed open from the inside.
- **The Hall of Lamps** — the keeper once said the first passage is lined with wall-lamps. Relight them and the barrow sleeps deeper.

What lies further in — an ossuary, flooded crypts, a gallery where the dead sing, a throne in the heart of the hill — is yours to find. The Delve Map panel records every room.

The barrow is asleep, but lightly. Fighting, forcing doors, shouting, raising light, or resting too long inside all make it more awake; relighting the lamps or slipping past quietly lets it settle again. When the Barrow Alarm reaches 6, the Barrow King opens his eyes.

## Rules at a Glance

- **Point-buy**: Might, Agility, Endurance, Wits, Insight, and Presence each start at 1. You spend 6 more points, up to 4 in any one attribute.
- **Checks**: d20 + the relevant attribute against a difficulty — easy 8, normal 12, hard 16, very hard 20. A natural 20 is a critical success, a natural 1 a critical failure. Start a check yourself from the Attribute checks panel, or roll any time with `/roll`.
- **Hit points**: at 0 you fall unconscious rather than dying outright, and a companion can drag you back.
- **Supplies**: a torch burns about an hour; rations, draughts, and silver live in your inventory. Without light you cannot see the way or the traps.
- **Panels**: quests, inventory, companion affinity, the Delve Map, the Barrow Alarm, the Lantern, and your Renown all update with the story.

## Notes for the Game Master

Tell it like a good GM: concrete scenes, brisk pacing, and the choice handed back to the player at the end of every turn. Call for a check only when the outcome is uncertain and failure has a cost, and name the attribute and difficulty first. Failure must move the story — spending time, torches, hit points, position, or trust — rather than stalling it. Danger is real but not gory; there is laughter in the tavern and singing in the tomb.

Reveal the truth of the barrow layer by layer, never all at once early on. No ending is fixed: returning the Lantern Heart, bargaining with the Barrow King, destroying him for good, or something the player invents should all be possible, and all of them should cost something.

## Opening

On a rainy late-autumn night you push open the door of the Crooked Antler. The reeve's bounty notice is nailed above the bar. In the corner an old soldier is cleaning his sword; by the window a young sister turns the pages of a book of lantern script. The innkeeper slides a hot mug of ale toward you.

"Here for the notice too?"
