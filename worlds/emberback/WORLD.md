# Emberback Relay

Vesper does not turn. One half of the planet faces a white sun that can soften metal; the other lies beneath permanent stars and moving ice. Life holds on in the Duskline between them, a narrow band of red stone, cold wind, and long shadows. Settlements survive because the relay chain tells them when heat fronts, ice squalls, and supply crawlers are coming.

Emberback Relay stands on a rust-colored basalt ridge at the chain's loneliest point. Its three masts connect six thousand people spread across hundreds of kilometers. The station is part workshop, part roadside town, and part community promise: no call goes unanswered. Water comes from night-facing condensers. Power comes from solar fields that must constantly be moved along the ridge. Every spare component has already lived two or three useful lives.

## How Emberback Works

The relay is run as a cooperative. Technicians, cooks, medics, drivers, and signal operators take the same maintenance shifts and argue at the same weekly table. Decisions that close a road or cut a settlement off the network are voted on in public, and the vote is logged. In an emergency the shift lead can act first and answer to the table afterward — a rule everyone respects until the morning it is used against them.

Power is the station's real currency. The solar fields feed one shared reserve, and everything draws from it: the long-range array, the storm shields that keep exposed electronics alive, the crawler chargers, the clinic, and the Nightglass link that carries Mina's research scans. Nothing important can run at full at the same time. Emberback's trade chits buy meals, parts, and route fares, but emergency care and emergency calls are never sold — they can only be limited by physics.

## The Places That Matter

- **The Relay Ridge** — three masts, the operations hall, and the long-range array on the tallest tower. The main tower is the only place a full-power transmission can be sent from, and the most dangerous place to stand when a storm arrives.
- **The Solar Fields** — panel rows on rails that crews drag along the ridge to chase the light. Moving them buys charge; doing it in a storm costs fingers.
- **The Condenser Yard** — the night-facing water plant, cold enough to fog your breath at noon, and the quietest place to talk.
- **Emberback Clinic** — eight beds, a cold room, and a reserve battery that keeps cryo-medicine and heat-stroke patients alive when the grid falters.
- **The Brightward Road** — the courier route toward the settlements under the sun. **Mile Seventeen** is an exposed crossing on it, with a field repeater bolted beneath a natural basalt arch.
- **Nightglass Observatory** — a cluster of domes on the night side where the instruments are colder than the people. It records Vesper's magnetic weather, and lately, signals that arrive before they are sent.

## The People

- **Tomas Reed** — fifty-eight, chief technician, elected shift lead, and your mentor. Patient, careful, famous for terrible coffee. He believes in verified evidence and public safety, and right now he has one hand on the emergency cutoff that would silence the network to protect it from Crownfire.
- **June Okafor** — thirty-one, the fastest Sunrunner courier on the Duskline. Funny until the danger is real, then exact. She is escorting a medical convoy of thirty cryo-packs toward Mile Seventeen — the place your future voice told you to keep it away from.
- **Dr. Mina Park** — forty-two, atmospheric physicist and head of Nightglass. She has spent months arguing that some echoes arrive before they are transmitted. Most operators call it instrument drift. This morning she is climbing the main tower with a storm reader, and your future self says to keep her off it.
- **Priya Nair** — forty-five, the clinic's lead medic. Practical, dry, and impossible to rush, she has two heat-stroke patients and a cold room full of medicine that cannot lose power. Every watt the array takes is a watt she has to argue for.
- **Eli Varga** — nineteen, the night-shift signal operator who logged the 06:14 call. Sharp with equipment, shaky under pressure, and certain he heard something in the static after your voice stopped. He has not told anyone yet.

## The Crownfire Morning

At 06:14 station time, every receiver in Emberback wakes at once. A voice cuts through the static. It is yours, exhausted and unmistakable, speaking from seventy-two hours in the future:

> Do not let the convoy reach Mile Seventeen. Keep Mina off the tower. And whatever Tomas tells you, do not shut down the signal.

The transmission repeats once, then the long-range network dies. On the brightward horizon a Crownfire storm turns the sky gold. It will reach the ridge in about three hours. As it approaches, radio reliability falls, exposed electronics become dangerous, and time-shifted echoes grow stronger.

The three warnings pull against each other. Stopping the convoy means getting a message to June without long-range comms — or driving out yourself on a crawler that needs charge. Keeping Mina off the tower means stopping the one person who might explain the call. And keeping the signal alive means keeping the array powered through a storm that can burn it out, against the judgment of the man who taught you everything.

## Echoes

An echo is evidence of a possible transmission, not a guaranteed timeline. Acting on one may prevent, alter, or accidentally cause what was heard. Every important signal has a source, channel, timestamp, carrier shape, and recording quality, and once a technical fact is established it stays established. Contradictions are clues, not mistakes. An impossible message needs a discoverable mechanism — a repeater, a storm fold, a recording loop — rather than narrative convenience.

## Story Shape

The story forces no single ending, but it tends to move through three stages:

1. **The Warning** — the first hours before Crownfire. Preserve the call, decide who to trust with it, and make the first hard power choices while the convoy rolls toward Mile Seventeen.
2. **The Storm** — Crownfire arrives. Comms fail and echoes multiply. The people you saved, persuaded, or overruled now hold the pieces of what actually happens at the tower and the arch.
3. **The Answer** — someone will have to send the call you received. Whether that is you, why the warnings were phrased that way, and what it costs to close the loop depends on every choice made before.

## Playing in Emberback

Emberback is a functioning community, not a cynical wasteland. People can be frightened, stubborn, wrong, or protective without becoming villains; conflict comes from competing responsibilities and incomplete information. Include practical cooperation, dry humor, and shared meals alongside danger.

Risky actions are resolved with dice: a d20 plus your relevant attribute against a stated difficulty, with natural 1s and 20s meaning something. A failed check should cost time, charge, equipment, trust, or position — never simply end the story.

The right-hand panels track what is at stake: the Crownfire countdown, the relay grid's reserve and which systems are powered, a structured log of every signal, and the convoy's position and cargo. The narrator reads those values; make your choices with them in view.

You are Emberback's newest linekeeper. You have a field radio, a crawler key, three hours of daylight, and a warning nobody else has heard in your voice. The future has made its call. What you do next decides who answers.
