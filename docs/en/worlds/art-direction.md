# World art direction

This page describes what the pictures for a world should look like, and what to check before you deliver them. It is written for the people who illustrate worlds: world authors, painters, and the people who maintain the built-in worlds. You do not need to know how to program.

It has three parts: how to set a direction for your own world, the direction set for each of the four built-in worlds, and how pictures appear in the world list (the gallery). For how to make the pictures, see [Illustrate characters](./portraits.md) and [Make scene pictures for a world](./scenes.md).

## Decide what the pictures are for first

A world's pictures have two jobs:

- **When the player chooses a world**, they show at a glance how the world is played.
- **After the player enters the world**, they make the characters, places, and rules look believable and consistent.

So different worlds should have different art styles. The four built-in worlds aim for the same level of finish, not the same way of drawing. When you set a direction for your own world, answer these four questions:

| Question                             | Example                                                                                                                                      |
| ------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| How is this world mainly played?     | Dialogue and relationships; investigation and risk; repair and survival                                                                      |
| What drawing style and which colors? | Clean anime line art, sea blue, mint green, cherry pink                                                                                      |
| How do the pictures mainly appear?   | Portraits with transparent backgrounds plus scene pictures (stage view); character cards with an environment plus one cover (text narrative) |
| What must never be drawn wrong?      | A character's injury is on the left arm; a building has three floors; a person is an adult                                                   |

The answer to the last question is called an **invariant**: the things that must stay the same no matter how many times you redraw or how many variants you make. Write them down, and check them against each picture after you make it.

- The names and narrative setting of 雾港 do not mean that the existing faces must be replaced, or that everyone must be changed to period costume.

## Visual invariants for each world

### 遥风学园

- Characters are told apart by hairstyle, expression, and one prop: 澪's manuscript paper, 凛's interview notebook and camera, 夏帆's acoustic guitar, 悠真's glasses and folder, 遥's schedule book, 千寻's chalk and old books, 奏太's drumsticks, and 茜's book and dried flowers.
- Keep the navy school uniform with white trim, which has already been finalized, and each person's existing silhouette. In expression variants, do not randomly change to a sailor uniform or a suit, and do not randomly change hair accessories or bows.
- 千寻 is an adult teacher in her twenties, and the students are high school students. The content should be clean and suitable for teenagers, but this refers to the standard for the content, not to drawing the teacher as a student. She must keep her adult identity, her professional clothing, and her gentle, guiding posture.
- The daytime scene is the master version of the space. A night scene changes only the light, the sky, and the on or off state of the existing lamps. The position of the camera, the horizon, doors and windows, floors, bookshelves, the road surface, and props do not change. Do not automatically add rain because it is night.
- Fixed features of the five scenes: the club building always has three floors, a tiled roof, and climbing vines on the right side. The window desks in the classroom and the notebook in the foreground are in fixed positions. The mezzanine and the long table on the right of the library are fixed. The vending machine on the right of the seawall slope is fixed. In the shopping street, the bookshop and the flower shop are on the left, and the restaurant and the instrument shop are on the right.
- The background is there for the characters to perform in. The foreground and the area for the dialogue box must be clearly visible. A night scene keeps a middle level of brightness. Do not turn a gentle everyday scene into a thriller.

### 雾港

- The identity of a place comes from the fog that never clears, the cliffs, the three-level port city, the rising and falling tide, and materials eroded by salt. The distance that can be seen in a scene should be short. Far away, only blurred outlines hint at things.
- The city panorama on the promotional cover is an overview of the genre. It does not mean that a player can see the whole city clearly in the fog, and it is not a route map of the ruins.
- Show the face and one signature prop first. Black cloth needs layers. Do not press every face into darkness for the sake of "darkness", and do not let ornate spells take attention away from clues and characters.
- 铁姑's fog-erosion is below the elbow of **her own left arm**, and her right arm is normal. Do not treat "the left side of the picture" as her left. It is partly translucent grey fog, not ice armor, lightning, or a mechanical prosthetic.
- When 灰隼 goes down at low tide, the light he carries is a shielded, low-brightness cool glow of tide stone, not an open flame. The warm lights of the safe places in the city and the exploration gear must be kept distinct.
- At the start, 小霜's fog-erosion only lets light through occasionally at her fingertips. 齐老's hands are much more translucent. Do not draw everyone as the same glowing mage. The exact degree follows the character card.
- Use clothing to show identity: the strict guild, the old authority of the council, the practicality and wear of 盐牙, and the plainness of the Fog Walkers. Keep the differences in age and experience. Do not make everyone a generic beauty.

### Emberback

- This is a planet that always keeps one face toward the sun. The bright side, the twilight band, and the dark side are areas of space, not time. Do not use the template of "a different sky for day and night". When a scene variant is needed later, prefer showing the state of storms, electricity, and communications.
- The identifying marks of the characters: Tomas's enamel mug and radio, June's scarf and car keys, Mina's spectrum analyzer. Keep each person's age, skin tone, face shape, and professional posture.
- What best expresses this world is: the ceramic heat shields of the relay station, equipment that has been repaired many times, analog gauges, six-wheeled crawler vehicles, and basalt landforms. Gears, armor, and space suits that have no use are not.
- The workshop, station yard, and observation deck around the characters are part of the character card. They are not transparent cut-out material for the stage. Do not cut out the environment just to make things tidy.
- Bright does not mean safe. Alarms, damaged equipment, the edge of a storm, and long shadows provide suspense, while the characters remain people that are easy to approach and part of a community.

### 提灯古冢

- Keep these: the village of 灰苇村, the northern burial mound, the ancient lamp at the mountaintop that has gone out, and the bronze tomb door standing open. A cold, damp late autumn, old bronze, and warm oil lamps are what set it apart from the cool tide-stone glow of 雾港.
- Keep the age, identity, and personality differences on the six character cards. 伊索德 is a woman in her seventies, 雷恩 is a nineteen-year-old apprentice priest, and 布兰诺克 is a fifty-year-old veteran.
- Full-body transparent portraits use the same frame size, similar figure size, and light from the same direction. The avatar and the stage portrait come from the same image.
- The landmark map shows only the rough relations between known places. It does not fix streets, distances, or the layout of the tomb that the source text does not write down. Lighting the lamp gallery is an exploration scene that may happen. It does not mean the lamps are already lit at the start.

## Making and delivering pictures

1. **Read the setting first, then look at the existing pictures.** Read `WORLD.md`, the character cards, and `data/dimensions.yaml`. If a prompt in the manifest contradicts an existing picture, fix the contradiction. Changing only the text does not mean the picture is acceptable.
2. **New characters and new places can be generated from the manifest.** The shared art style description in the manifest only reduces drift in style. It does not guarantee that the same person looks the same, or that the same place has the same layout.
3. **For a variant of an existing character, or another time of day for an existing scene, use the finalized picture as a reference.** List each thing that must not change. The batch scripts in the repository generate from text only, and they do not read reference pictures.
4. **Size.** Character pictures are `1024x1536`. Scene pictures are `1536x1024`. A transparent portrait must have truly transparent pixels. A transparency channel that is fully opaque does not count. Painting a checkerboard into the picture does not count either.
5. **Format.** Portraits and scenes are stored only as WebP (lossy, quality 90, with a lossless transparency channel). The generation scripts write WebP directly. For a PNG made with another tool, convert it with the command below, and then delete the PNG. This needs `cwebp` installed on your computer.

   ```bash
   node scripts/encode-world-webp.mjs <file.png>...
   ```

6. **Check each picture.** View it at full size, view the thumbnail, place it on a light background and a dark background, check how it is cropped at phone width, and compare the day and night versions. The picture must not contain explanatory text, watermarks, or signs that have not been proofread. Text that must appear is best left to the interface to display.
7. **Before you replace a finalized picture, keep the original or the Git history.** After the chosen picture is put into the world package, regenerate the corresponding manifest. See the next section.

Some of the pictures finalized this round were made by "editing an image with an image": the night pictures of the five scenes in 遥风学园, and the corrections to the four character pictures of 雾港. Their full prompts and the reference picture versions used are recorded in the [prompt record](../../world-art-prompts.json).

### What to refresh when a picture changes

When a portrait or scene file changes (redrawn, replaced, or recompressed), the checksum recorded for it in the manifest is out of date, even if the picture looks the same. Regenerate it:

```bash
node scripts/emit-presence.mjs mistport
node scripts/emit-scenes.mjs haruka-academy
pnpm validate:world worlds/emberback worlds/mistport worlds/haruka-academy worlds/lantern-barrow
```

When all validation passes, each world prints one line:

```text
✓ worlds/emberback/world.yaml
✓ worlds/mistport/world.yaml
✓ worlds/haruka-academy/world.yaml
✓ worlds/lantern-barrow/world.yaml
```

When you change a built-in world, run the picture-asset test again. It checks that every language of each world refers to the current pictures:

```bash
pnpm --filter @covel/server exec vitest run tests/lib/world-art-assets.test.ts
```

The gallery manifest `gallery.json` also records the checksum and size of each picture. It must be updated in the same way.

Some rules about files:

- Portraits and scenes keep only WebP. PNG files in these two folders are ignored by Git and are not committed.
- Do not compress a WebP that was already compressed with loss again. When you change a picture, convert it again from a source that was never compressed: the original generated image, or the PNG in the Git history.
- Gallery pictures are an exception: they keep the original PNG, with a WebP of the same name beside it for display. The desktop installer and the Docker image do not include the gallery PNGs. So every PNG in `gallery.json` must have a `webFilename`.

### Cover

A world's cover is declared by two fields in `world.yaml`:

```yaml
cover: media/gallery/world-cover.webp
accentColor: "oklch(72% 0.15 350)"
```

See the [world data reference](../reference/world-data.md#background-music) for the formats and lightness range of `accentColor`. Loading discards invalid colours, so the world still loads and the interface derives its default accent from the world ID. `pnpm validate:world` still reports an error for the author to fix. The interface also uses the default accent when stored metadata contains an unusable colour.

- `cover` is the path of one picture, and it must be in a folder under `media/`. The interface shows this picture directly. If a character picture in the world package is updated, the cover does not change automatically.
- If `cover` is not set, the interface uses the default background.
- If the cover fits the play and the quality is acceptable, you can keep using it.

### Players see the pictures before the game starts

The pictures in a world package are shown before the player enters the game:

- The world detail page lists all the pictures.
- Some interface styles show scene pictures and stills in a full-screen background carousel, place the character pictures below the introduction, and let you click to see the original and its description.
- Some interface styles show a few avatars and the total number of pictures in the lower-left corner of the cover, and cycle through the scene pictures when the mouse hovers over the cover.

So every picture must be able to stand on its own. Do not put unfinished work into a world package.

Which pictures are shown, what they are called, and how they are described is decided by the gallery manifest below.

## Gallery data and front-end use

The gallery is the group of pictures shown in the world list and the world details. It is decided by `media/gallery.json` in the world package. Translations go in the neighboring `gallery.en-US.json` or `gallery.zh-CN.json`.

**This file is optional.** In that case, the gallery lists the pictures in the public picture folders of the world package (portraits, scenes). It tells scene and character pictures apart by aspect ratio, and it has no names or descriptions. If you write `gallery.json`, you decide the order, the categories, and the description of each picture.

The gallery and the portraits and scenes in the game are two different things. Which portrait and which background are used in the game is still decided by the manifests of [Portraits](./portraits.md) and [Scenes](./scenes.md). The gallery does not change any state in the game.

### How the gallery uses this manifest

- It shows the pictures in the order of the manifest, up to 60.
- If `webFilename` is set, it shows that. If not, it shows `filename`.
- It shows only pictures whose `spoilerLevel` is `opening` or is not set. The gallery is seen before playing begins, so it does not show pictures that spoil the story.
- `filename` and `webFilename` must be written as "a one-level folder under `media/` / file name", for example `gallery/harbor.webp`. Pictures written in any other form are not shown.
- When the manifest file itself is malformed, the gallery does not come out empty. It falls back to the "no such file" approach.

`kind` decides how a picture is used:

| `kind`     | Use                                                                                                                                               |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `scene`    | Scene picture. It fills the screen in a carousel, and cropping removes one strip from the top and one from the bottom                             |
| `still`    | Still from the story. Same as above                                                                                                               |
| `map`      | Map or landmark diagram. Shown only in full, and not part of the carousel                                                                         |
| `portrait` | Character picture                                                                                                                                 |
| `hero`     | The world's own cover picture. A world with no built-in cover in the app (for example, a world a player installed) uses it as its opening picture |

### Fields of each picture record

| Field                                | Meaning                                                                                                                                |
| ------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `id`, `kind`                         | A fixed identifier, and the category from the table above                                                                              |
| `name`, `description`                | The name shown, and a short description of the picture                                                                                 |
| `background`                         | Background material. It must be traceable in the world text or the character card                                                      |
| `storyContext`                       | What story situation this picture fits, and what plot result must not be inferred from the picture                                     |
| `location`, `visualNotes`            | A place that is supported by evidence, and visual points confirmed by looking at each picture. Character cards may leave these out     |
| `relatedCharacters`                  | IDs of related characters. Listing one does not mean that character must appear in the picture                                         |
| `depiction`, `spoilerLevel`          | Whether this is a character portrait, a setting illustration, a landmark diagram, or a moment that may happen, and how much it reveals |
| `sourceRefs`                         | Paths of the original setting material in the world package                                                                            |
| `filename`, `webFilename`            | The picture file relative to `media/`, and an optional WebP display version                                                            |
| `width`, `height`, `sha256`, `bytes` | The size, checksum, and size in bytes of the file named by `filename`                                                                  |
| `generation`                         | An optional generation record: the tool used, the full prompt, and the reference pictures. It is not background material for players   |

The interface actually reads `id`, `kind`, `filename`, `webFilename`, `name`, `description`, `background`, `location`, and `spoilerLevel`. The other fields are editing material for the people who maintain the worlds.

### Rules for writing descriptions

- The main file contains only text in the world's default language. Language files contain only translations, matched to the main file by `id`. Picture paths, character IDs, and checksum information are written only in the main file.
- Setting fields must be traceable to the source text. If the source does not say something, leave it empty. Do not fill in character relationships, backgrounds, ages, or plot results with "reasonable guesses".
- Composition, clothing, and appearance written in a prompt for the sake of the picture cannot be used as evidence for the world setting.
- Expressions, clothing, and day-and-night variants are described separately. Do not infer a relationship or an event from one person's expression.
- A still uses the original character portrait as the reference for identity. Do not write an activity in the picture as something that has already happened.
- When an existing picture has no reliable generation record, do not write `generation`. Do not pass off the creative intent in the manifest as an actual generation record.

## Art worth investing in next

Below are suggestions that have not been verified. They are not content that has been finished or connected.

| World     | What to add first                                                                                                                                              | How to tell whether it helps                                                                                           |
| --------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 遥风学园  | Restrained emotional variants of 澪, 悠真, and 茜, and changes to the decorations on the eve of the school festival                                            | Can a player, without reading labels, recognize the same person, the emotion, and how far the festival has progressed? |
| 雾港      | Clue illustrations such as the main pier, the guild appraisal table, and the low-tide entrance. Use the tide level rather than day and night to show the state | Can a player see what is being investigated, where the safe boundary is, and the pressure to get out?                  |
| Emberback | The signal repair room, the crawler station yard, and Nightglass. Use normal, power cut, and approaching storm to show the state                               | Can a player understand the state of the equipment and where they can go next?                                         |

The suggested order is: first look at the reasons that players with different interests choose these worlds, and then look at whether, once inside, they feel the game matches what they expected, and whether they want to finish the first goal. An aesthetic judgment cannot replace this evidence. Do not change a world's original play just to fill up the number of pictures in a common visual-novel set.

## Checklist before delivery

| What to check               | What a passing result looks like                                                                                               |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Matches the setting         | Age, identity, props, and place features all agree with the character card and the world text. Each invariant has been checked |
| Consistent within one world | The drawing style, tone, and direction of light are the same. People are similar in size and framing                           |
| Size and format             | Characters `1024x1536`, scenes `1536x1024`, WebP. Pictures that require transparency are really transparent                    |
| Recognizable when small     | When shrunk to an avatar, you can still tell who it is                                                                         |
| Different backgrounds       | A transparent portrait placed on light and dark backgrounds has no white or black edge                                         |
| No extra text               | No watermarks, explanatory text, or garbled signs                                                                              |
| The manifests are current   | After changing a picture, `emit-presence` / `emit-scenes` has been run again, and validation passes                            |
| Clean folders               | No PNG is left in `media/portraits/` or `media/scenes/`                                                                        |
